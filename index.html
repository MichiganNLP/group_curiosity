02 — What we found

Curiosity helps most
when the answer can expand.

Across model families, curiosity-guided groups outperform non-curious groups on seven of eight tasks. The largest benefits appear in creative and social settings; tightly constrained reasoning exposes the cost of too much discussion.

B

Allocation matters

All curious for divergence.
One curious for focus.

Multiple question-asking agents help story generation and stereotype mitigation. One curious agent often works better for Codenames, hypothesis generation, and Novel Concepts, where coordination costs rise quickly.

Culture is selective

Perspective widens the space—sometimes too far.

Country-conditioned agents improve many creative and social outcomes, but can pull precision-oriented reasoning away from a narrow correct answer. Cultural grounding is a prompt-based proxy, not a representation of real people.

03 — Design lessons

Ask when asking
earns its cost.

Curiosity introduces useful information and communication overhead at the same time. Good multi-agent design allocates questions according to the task rather than treating more interaction as universally better.

01

Match curiosity to the task

Use broader questioning for divergent, open-ended work; use a single questioner when the group must converge.

02

Separate quality from tokens

Multi-agent curiosity offers strong quality-per-token on creative and social tasks, while single agents remain efficient for factual reasoning.

03

Ground culture carefully

Country prompts can activate diverse priors, but they simplify culture and can reproduce broad generalizations if used uncritically.
